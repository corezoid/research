# Corezoid research corpus

The corpus of Alexander Vityaz (founder of Corezoid Inc.; ORCID 0009-0006-0489-7881): peer-citable
papers, a book in progress, interviews, historical press releases, and patents. It is the
theoretical basis of Corezoid (process engine) and Simulator.Company (executable digital twin of an
organisation built on Actor Graphs).

## Where things are

Paths below and in the references are relative to the **corpus root**: the `corpus/` folder next to
this file if it exists (bundled install), otherwise the repository root two levels up (`../../`).

- [references/index.md](references/index.md) — every work with DOI, abstract, keywords and what it
  builds on. **Read this first** for any substantive question; it is generated from the corpus and
  always current.
- [references/glossary.md](references/glossary.md) — the corpus's own terms, each with the work that
  defines it.
- Full texts — open the `text` path given in the index only for the works you need. `paper.md`
  where it exists; otherwise `paper.pdf` (formulas and figures are authoritative in the PDF either way).

## How to answer

1. Find the relevant works in the index (by title, keywords, abstract), then read the actual text
   before stating what a paper proves or defines. The abstract is not enough for a claim about a
   theorem, its assumptions or its scope.
2. Keep the genres apart:
   - **Papers** are the results. Cite them by version DOI: `Vityaz, A. (2026). Title. DOI`.
   - **Codex of Actors** chapters are a work in progress; cite as a chapter of the book in progress.
   - **Interviews and press releases** are primary sources — what the author said or did at the
     time, in that period's terminology. Use them for history and motivation, never as evidence for
     a formal result. Most are unofficial English translations; say so when quoting.
   - **Patents** document the engineering lineage (universal computing elements → actor graph
     engine); cite the patent number.
3. Separate what the corpus proves from what it conjectures or argues. The papers label this
   themselves (theorem vs. conjecture vs. architectural principle vs. essay) — keep the label.
4. Do not attribute to the author a claim, number or quotation that is not in the texts. If the
   corpus does not cover something, say so rather than extrapolating.
5. Use the corpus's terms as defined in the glossary (e.g. *register*, *Actor Graph*,
   *management debt*). When a term has a narrower formal meaning than its everyday one, use the
   formal one or flag the difference.
6. Answer in the user's language; keep titles, terms and quotations in their original form.

## Reading order for newcomers

1. *How to Become a Smart Company* — plain-language essay, the whole argument in brief.
2. *A Phase Model of Enterprise Evolution* and *Company Brain* — the architecture: fragmented company
   → digital core → digital twin → autonomous enterprise.
3. *On the Necessity of Noise Suppression for Minimal Good Regulators* — the cybernetic theorem the
   rest relies on.
4. *Active Transaction Graphs* and *Actor Graphs* — the formal model.
5. Then by interest: the firm (*Computable Boundary*, *Compact Company*, *Management Debt*), work and
   cognition (*What Is Work*, *Metaunderstanding*, *Quantum Learning*), software (*Beyond Programming
   Languages*), foundations (*Ontology of Transition*).
