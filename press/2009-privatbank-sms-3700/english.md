# Alexander Vityaz presented PrivatBank’s 12-language 3700 SMS Information service

**Source:** OBOZ.UA / Obozrevatel · **Published:** February 3, 2009, 15:57

By sending an SMS to 3700, anyone could receive an answer to virtually any question.

The message was charged at the mobile operator’s standard rate, while the service operated in 12 languages and was available from anywhere in the world.

PrivatBank’s new service had already been dubbed a “killer service” for fee-based information providers. The bank’s team had configured the system so that messages cost only the operator’s standard rate, with not a cent added, while high-quality answers arrived by SMS within minutes.

“A similar service has been operating at the bank since 2005, but previously we answered only questions related to banking and banking-related topics. We have now taken it further because we can see that people have many questions on other subjects,” Alexander Vityaz, Head of PrivatBank’s Electronic Business Centre, wrote in his personal blog. “We currently process thousands of SMS questions every day.”

The 3700 service understood questions phrased in natural language. Unlike internet search queries, which often required users to type something like “Kyiv cinema today,” an SMS could contain a complete, natural question: “Which cinema in Kyiv is showing a good comedy after 9:00 p.m.?”

The new service also benefited the bank’s corporate clients. If someone asked, for example, where to rent a car in Lviv, the bank knew which companies in the region offered that service and could send a prompt reply by SMS. In such cases, PrivatBank drew the information from its extensive database of services provided by corporate customers.

Another feature was multilingual support. The 3700 service answered messages in 12 languages: Ukrainian, Russian, English, German, Portuguese, Italian, Spanish, Latvian, Polish, Greek, Georgian, and Hebrew.

The SMS answers expanded PrivatBank’s range of mobile services. At the time of publication, the bank’s mobile-banking services allowed customers to check account balances, pay utility bills, block a card, purchase mobile-phone and Skype vouchers, and send money transfers.
