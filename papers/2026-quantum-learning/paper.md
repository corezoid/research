# Quantum Learning

## A Concept for a New Educational System: From Educational Programs to Managing Changes in Human Capability

Alexander Vityaz · *Corezoid Inc., Dnipro, Ukraine* · ORCID: [0009-0006-0489-7881](https://orcid.org/0009-0006-0489-7881)

## Abstract

The modern educational system is organized around large, preassembled units: the course, discipline, semester, program, qualification, and degree. This architecture emerged in a world where knowledge changed relatively slowly, professions remained stable, and professional capabilities had long life cycles. Today, technologies and workforce requirements change faster than universities can update their programs. Employers need a continuous reskilling system, while working adults need a precise way to close the gap between their current capabilities and the next task. Learning becomes their third job, alongside paid employment and family responsibilities. At the labor-market level, this gap appears as structural unemployment: vacancies and job seekers coexist, but the graphs of required and available capabilities do not match. Artificial intelligence accelerates this mismatch by automating some classes of tasks while intensifying labor shortages in others. This paper proposes the concept of quantum learning: an event-driven, discrete, continuous, and executable system for managing changes in human capability. Its fundamental unit is the learning quantum—a minimal, completed, and verified transition from one capability state to another. Quantum learning replaces the linear educational program with an individual graph of transitions. Students, teachers, mentors, experts, employers, and educational organizations are represented by bounded digital twins. Tasks, capabilities, quanta, attempts, errors, evidence, and authorizations exist as autonomous actors in an executable environment. Changing the unit of learning also changes the economics of education. A student pays for the individual pathway actually completed, while capabilities already demonstrated reduce its duration and price. The university moves from a one-time sale of a degree to the continuous stewardship of the graphs of people and companies; the teacher scales expertise through reusable quanta, simulations, AI, and work on complex exceptions. Simulator is the Corezoid Inc. platform on which the author is implementing this architecture. The concept itself is technologically neutral and can be implemented in any environment capable of executing an event-driven graph of actors, states, and transitions. The primary learner for whom this system is designed is the working adult, who simultaneously carries three jobs: paid employment, family responsibilities, and continuous learning. 

**Keywords:** quantum learning; capability-based education; learning graphs; digital twins; event-driven systems; workforce reskilling; structural unemployment 

_A course measures content. A quantum measures change in the person._ 

## Part I. Foundations of Quantum Learning 

1. Education Can No Longer Keep Pace with the World 

The world changes faster than a person can complete an educational program. Professions are reconfigured faster than universities revise their disciplines. Tools become obsolete faster than employees complete corporate courses. Employers need new capabilities now, while the educational system offers semesters, programs, and diplomas months or years later. Studying for three, four, or five years before beginning to act is too slow. Retraining for months every time a technology, process, or role changes is also too slow. 

The problem is not a shortage of teachers, platforms, video lectures, or educational content. The problem lies in the architecture of education itself. It is still built around large, preassembled packages: 

- a course • a program 

- • a discipline • a degree • a semester • a diploma. 

The content is predetermined. The duration is fixed in advance. The sequence of topics is set in advance. Completion of the program is treated as the result, rather than a demonstrated change in what the person can do. This model was created for a world in which knowledge changed relatively slowly, professions lasted for decades, and learning could be separated from work. 

That world no longer exists. 

#### 1.1. Learning Has Become a Third Job 

_“A working adult today has not two jobs, but three. The first is paid work. The second is family—children, loved ones, the home, and pets. The third is continuous learning. The world is changing, and this is permanent. Learning therefore cannot be designed as an unpaid add-on to evenings and weekends.”_ — Alexander Vityaz 

An adult’s first job is paid professional work. The second is family: children, loved ones, older relatives, domestic responsibilities, and pets. Continuous learning becomes a third job and competes with the first two for time, attention, energy, and recovery. It cannot be designed as an optional hobby after the working day. When an organization requires continuous capability renewal, it must account for the learning load in working time, funding, role planning, and the evaluation of results. A quantum architecture must reduce the cost of this third job without damaging the first two: it must recognize what has already been demonstrated, remove unnecessary segments of the pathway, connect learning to a real task, support flexible formats, and introduce human assistance only where it is genuinely necessary. 

Here, “job” means a persistent circuit of obligations requiring time, attention, planning, and responsibility. The concept is broader than paid employment: family, care, the home, pets, and learning also create recurring demands that compete for an adult’s limited resources.

#### 1.2. Structural Unemployment: Jobs and People Exist, but the Transition Does Not 

Artificial intelligence does not merely reduce or increase the total number of jobs. It changes the structure of labor demand. Demand for some office, administrative, and content-related functions may decline, while manufacturing, construction, energy, logistics, maintenance, and other sectors simultaneously face labor shortages. A review published by _Ekonomichna Pravda_ on July 22, 2026, describes this process as a deepening of structural unemployment in Ukraine: AI reduces demand for some classes of labor while intensifying shortages in others [2]. 

This is a structural problem, not merely a cyclical one. Vacancies exist. Job seekers also exist. But the sets of capabilities, work contexts, authorizations, geographic conditions, physical constraints, and terms of employment do not match. The International Labour Organization defines skills mismatch precisely as a discrepancy between the capabilities employers seek and those individuals possess [3]. A copywriter cannot replace a welder. Releasing a person from one occupation does not automatically turn that person into a resource for another industry. Labor is not a homogeneous mass that can be mechanically reassigned from one vacancy to another. 

Every vacancy is a graph of tasks, required capabilities, tools, risks, and authorizations. Every person is another graph: demonstrated capabilities, transferable experience, constraints, goals, and possible next transitions. Structural unemployment arises when these graphs do not match and the system cannot compute and execute a transition between them. The traditional educational system responds to such a gap with another long program. But a person whose skills have lost market relevance today, and an employer with an unfilled role today, do not need a generic course. #### 4.1. Being Able to Solve Specific Problems: The “Mathematical Trivium” 

In 1991, V. I. Arnold published _The Mathematical Trivium_ —a list of one hundred relatively simple problems intended as a necessary minimum of practical mathematical qualification. His criterion was operational: a specialist is defined not by the complexity of the terminology they command, but by the problems they can solve with their own hands [6]. 

_“What is at issue here is not any difficult problem, but the simple questions that constitute the strictly necessary minimum. There need not be many such problems, but the ability to solve them must be required rigorously.”_ — V. I. Arnold, _The Mathematical Trivium_ , 1991 

Quantum learning generalizes this principle beyond mathematics. Every qualification should have its own professional trivium: a compact and explicit set of classes of tasks that a person must be able to perform independently, at a specified level of quality. This is not a complete curriculum, but a strict operational minimum. 

A university must answer not only the question “Which topics were studied?” but, above all, “Which specific tasks can this person perform, in what context, with what degree of independence, and on the basis of what evidence?” For a procedural capability, one rigorous practical assessment may be sufficient evidence; for professional judgment, a series of decisions across multiple contexts may be required. A qualification claim without a task and a result remains insufficient. 

### 5. The State of a Capability 

A person does not simply “have” or “lack” a capability. A capability has a state. 

For example: 

_Not diagnosed → deficit identified → understands the principle → performs by following an example → performs with a prompt → performs under supervision → performs independently → transfers to a new context → can assess others → reverification required._ 

A capability state is described not by a single grade, but by a set of parameters: action, context, quality of the result, independence, assistance used, and currency of the evidence. 

This model makes it possible to replace vague lists of skills with observable states. 

### 6. Learning Begins with a Gap 

Traditional education begins with a program. Quantum learning begins with the gap between what a person is required to do and what that person can do now. Let: 

- _S_ c be the current capability state 

- _S_ r be the state required by the task. 

The gap is recorded as a structural difference between the required and current capability states. For each component, the system must specify what is missing: knowledge of a rule, recognition of a situation, quality, independence, speed, transfer to a new context, or the necessary authorization. A gap may arise because a person: 

- does not know the rule 

- does not understand the context 

- does not recognize the situation 

- cannot select the appropriate action 

- uses an obsolete tool 

   - does not meet the quality criterion 

   - cannot transfer the capability to a new context 

   - makes a critical error 

   - performs the action too slowly 

   - is not ready for the required level of risk. 

- can act only with prompting 

Once the gap has been identified, the system must generate not an entire course, but the minimal transition required to close it. 

### 7. Learning Is Triggered by an Event 

Traditional learning is triggered by a schedule. Quantum learning is triggered by an event. An event may be: 

- the emergence of a new task • an employee’s transition to another • expiration of the capability’s validity 

- • a change in a work process role period • the introduction of a new tool • an error • the emergence of a new professional • goal 

- • a change in legislation a decline in quality • 

- • a change in employer requirements. an unsuccessful attempt 

The event creates a new requirement or reveals a mismatch between the task and the person’s current state. The system must then: 

1. identify the required capability 

2. retrieve the current state 

3. compute the gap

Learning ceases to be a separate activity. 

It becomes part of the activity itself. 

## Part II. Digital Twins and Actors in the Educational System 

8. Quantum Learning Is Impossible Without Digital Twins 

Quantum learning cannot be implemented through a content library, a course management system, or a chatbot alone. Managing a transition requires more than a single student profile. It requires an executable graph of bounded digital twins. The human actors are the student, teacher, mentor, expert, employer, and educational organization. The executable entities are the task, capability, gap, quantum, attempt, error, evidence, simulation, assessment rule, and authorization. AI is also an actor, with explicitly defined powers. 

This is not a collection of records. Every actor has a state, receives events, performs actions, creates evidence, and affects the decisions of other actors. The model stores only the data required for a specific transition and its assessment ([Active Transaction Graphs](../2026-active-transaction-graphs/) [9]). 

9. The Conant–Ashby Theorem as an Architectural Foundation 

The need for current models of actors is motivated by more than the convenience of automation. 

The Conant–Ashby Good Regulator Theorem establishes a general requirement for any system of regulation [1]: 

_Every good regulator of a system must be a model of that system._ 

Every participant in the learning process performs a regulatory function. The student regulates their own actions. The teacher regulates the learning intervention. The mentor regulates the transition toward independence. 

The expert regulates the verification of a complex capability. The employer regulates requirements and authorization for real work. The educational organization regulates resources, trajectories, and the quality of transitions. AI regulates the local selection of explanations, exercises, and feedback. 

Every regulator must possess a sufficiently rich model of the system about which it makes decisions. The teacher must have a model of the student. The student must have a model of the task, the goal, and the criteria for the result. The employer must have a model of the required role and the person’s demonstrated readiness. 

The educational organization must have models of students, teachers, employers, tasks, quanta, and its own capabilities. AI must receive from Simulator the current states of all actors whose transition it helps organize. Without a model, the regulator decides blindly. With an outdated model, it regulates a past state. 

With a model that is too coarse, it cannot distinguish the system’s consequential states. 

_No model of an actor—no good regulation of that actor. No graph of digital twins—no governable educational system._ 

The theorem does not imply that a particular platform or a single model format is mandatory. The engineering implication follows from the law of requisite variety: when tasks, roles, and human states change faster than an administrator can update static profiles, the model must update in response to events, remain machine-readable, and participate in the execution of decisions. 

A digital twin is the proposed technical implementation of this requirement. Its quality is assessed not by the volume of data collected, but by the accuracy of decisions, the speed of adaptation, and the capacity to correct its own errors.

### 10. The Student as an Actor 

In Simulator, each student is represented as a distinct actor with a unique actor_id. The student’s digital twin persists across the educational and professional trajectory, but does not claim to describe the person’s identity or personality. The model connects demonstrated capabilities, goals, roles, work contexts, current authorizations, unresolved gaps, and the history of semantically significant learning events. It records not only the result, but also the degree of independence and the assistance without which the result would not have been achieved. 

The student is not a passive object of intervention. The student accepts or rejects a goal, selects an available pathway, requests assistance, submits evidence, contests an interpretation, and controls data disclosure. The digital twin must strengthen the student’s agency, not replace it. 

### 11. The Teacher as an Actor 

The teacher must not exist in the system merely as a name on a schedule. The teacher’s digital twin connects the domain of expertise, the authority to define and verify capabilities, the quanta created, the methods used, diagnostic decisions, and students’ actual results. The teacher’s function changes. Instead of repeating the same content for every cohort, the teacher designs capability models, simulations, and criteria; analyzes complex errors; mentors students; and develops the system of transitions itself. AI and Simulator assume routine adaptation and first-line feedback. 

When a quantum designed by a teacher systematically fails to produce the stated transition, the system must 

revise not only the student model, but also the models of the teacher, the method, the simulation, and the quantum itself. 

### 12. The Mentor and the Expert 

The mentor regulates the transition from assisted action to independent performance. The result is not a task completed jointly, but a reduction in the person’s dependence on prompts and intervention. The system therefore records the form of assistance, the moment of intervention, and the development of independence. The expert enters the process where an automated rule cannot reliably assess professional judgment, an ambiguous result, or a critical capability. The expert verifies the decision within their domain of competence and assumes responsibility for the qualification claim. 

Human assessment is not treated as infallible. The digital twins of mentors and experts include the boundaries of their authority, decision histories, disagreements, reviews, and the outcomes of appeals. 

### 13. The Employer as an Active Participant in Learning 

The employer is not an external customer who appears only after the student graduates. The employer is a full actor in the learning process. The employer provides the system with its most important information: 

- which tasks actually exist 

- which processes are changing 

- which capabilities are required 

- under what conditions they are applied 

- which errors are tolerable 

   - what level of independence is required 

   - which tools are used 

   - what quality of result is sufficient 

   - when a previously demonstrated capability becomes obsolete. 

- which errors are critical 

#### 13.1. The Employer Creates Requirements 

A change in technology, process, product, legislation, or organizational role must automatically change the model of required capabilities. The employer should not send a university a list of competencies once every several years. 

Its digital twin continuously publishes current requirements arising from actual work. 

#### 13.2. The Employer Supplies Tasks 

Learning quanta should be based not only on artificial assignments, but also on real or anonymized work situations: 

- documents 

- operational data 

- projects 

- incidents 

- errors 

- decisions 

- process changes

#### 13.3. The Employer Provides the Context for Simulation 

The employer’s digital twin makes it possible to reproduce: 

- the process structure • constraints 

- • roles • tools • input data • rules 

- possible errors 

- the consequences of decisions 

- criteria for the result. 

This model is used to create a safe simulation of real activity. 

#### 13.4. The Employer Participates in Assessment 

The learning system can verify an action in a simulation. But transfer of the capability to real work must be verified by an actual result. Assessment may include: 

1. a learning exercise 

2. a simulation 

3. performance under observation 

4. limited authorization 

5. independent performance 

6. employer confirmation of a sustained result. 

#### 13.5. The Employer Manages Authorization 

A demonstrated capability must affect a person’s real rights: 

- to observe 

- to perform with prompting 

- to perform under supervision 

   - to perform a higher-risk task 

   - to assess the actions of others 

   - to modify the process itself. 

- to perform a standard task independently 

The employer determines the level of evidence required for each authorization. 

#### 13.6. The Employer Closes the Feedback Loop 

When an employee successfully completes a learning quantum but cannot perform the real task, the defect may lie in: 

- the capability model 

- the simulation 

- the assessment criterion 

- the learning quantum 

- the teacher’s evaluation 

- the employer’s requirements. 

The actual work result must return to Simulator and change the models of all connected actors. The employer is not a consumer of the educational result, but a co-author and active regulator of the learning process.

### 14. The Educational Organization as an Actor 

A university or corporate learning system must have its own digital twin. This requirement follows from the Good Regulator Theorem and from the need to maintain a current model of the organization’s own capabilities, constraints, and results. If an organization attempts to manage changes in capability without models of: 

- students 

- teachers 

- employers 

- tasks 

- capabilities 

- learning quanta 

- assessments 

- its own resources and constraints, 

then it is not a good regulator. It may conduct classes, assign tasks, administer examinations, and issue diplomas, but it cannot demonstrate that it manages changes in capability. The educational organization’s digital twin must represent: 

- which transitions it can provide

### 15. Why an LMS Is Not a Digital Twin 

A modern learning management system usually stores: 

- a list of students 

- a course catalog 

- materials 

- a schedule 

- attendance 

- grades 

- test results 

- certificates. 

This is an administrative model of educational activity. It shows what was assigned to a person and what the person formally completed. But it does not show: 

- what the person can do 

- how the person’s capability changed 

- which intervention caused the change 

- what assistance the person used 

- the context in which the result was achieved 

- how durable the result is

An LMS represents the document flow of the old educational system. A digital twin must model the causal circuit through which capabilities change.

### 16. The Task as an Actor 

A task is more than a textual description. As an actor, it contains the goal, input data, required result, deadline, constraints, risk, admissible assistance, tools, assessment rules, and consequences of error. 

The task connects the employer’s digital twin with the student’s digital twin. A person’s readiness is always computed relative to a specific task, not an abstract occupational title. 

### 17. The Capability as an Actor 

Every consequential capability is represented by an operational model: action, object, context, tools, constraints, quality criteria, complexity, independence, admissible assistance, verification rules, and period of validity. 

The capability connects task requirements, the student’s state, learning interventions, attempts, assessments, evidence, and authorizations. A skill label without these connections is not a sufficient model. 

### 18. The Gap as an Actor 

A gap must not remain a textual recommendation generated by AI. It exists as an explicit difference between the current and target states of a specific capability, tied to a person, task, time, risk, and closure criterion. 

##### **Example of a gap** 

The student can construct a financial model from a prepared template, but the task requires the student to select the structure independently and test the robustness of the assumptions. 

This is not a generic “gap in financial modeling.” It is the precise reason why the person cannot yet receive the required authorization. 

### 19. The Learning Quantum as an Executable Actor 

In Simulator, a learning quantum is not a fragment of content but an executable transition process. It connects the initial and target states, the intervention, the person’s action, admissible assistance, branches following an error, assessment rules, and the required evidence. 

An explanation, example, exercise, simulation, prompt, or consultation may form part of a quantum, but none of these elements is a quantum on its own. The criterion of a quantum is a demonstrated change of state, not the small size of the material. 

### 20. The Attempt as an Actor 

A capability cannot be assessed solely from the final answer. A semantically significant attempt records the sequence of actions, tools used, interactions with AI, mentor assistance, intermediate decisions, time, errors, corrections, and assessment result. The final answer may be identical while the capability state differs: the person may have acted independently, after one prompt, after a complete explanation, or with the aid of an AI system that effectively solved the task on the person’s behalf. 

At the same time, every click, pause, and draft must not become a permanent part of the digital twin. The level of detail and retention period are determined by the purpose, risk, and rules of the private learning circuit. 

### 21. The Error as an Actor 

An error must not disappear inside a negative grade. A consequential error becomes an event when it changes the learning pathway, risk level, or authorization decision; it records the expected and actual action, context, consequences, and hypotheses about causes. 

An error may indicate not only the student’s state, but also a defect in the task, instruction, simulation, criterion, teacher, employer model, or educational process itself. Its function is therefore to trigger diagnosis and correction, not automatic punishment. 

### 22. Evidence as an Actor 

Every verified transition must have evidence: the result of real work, an action in a simulation, a document, a software artifact, a decision made, a series of consistently performed operations, an automated assessment, an expert evaluation, or a combination of these. 

**Evidence specifies** what is being demonstrated, who acted, in what context, with what assistance, against which criteria, by whom it was assessed, when it was obtained, and where it applies. A certificate may be generated from evidence. A certificate does not replace evidence. 

### 23. Authorization as an Actor 

Authorization determines which actions a person has the right to perform in a real system. It is created on the basis of evidence and may be expanded, restricted, suspended, revoked, or made subject to reverification. 

Authorization connects learning with activity and risk. The complete progression from observation to the right to modify the process is presented in Section 26.

### 27. The Individual Learning Trajectory Is a Graph 

A student’s learning trajectory is not a linear sequence of courses, modules, and semesters. It is an individual graph of states and transitions. Its nodes are: 

- capability states 

- goals 

- tasks 

- gaps 

- learning quanta 

- attempts 

- errors 

- evidence 

- authorizations 

- work outcomes. 

Its edges are transitions: 

- acquisition of a capability 

- increased independence 

- transfer to a new context 

- correction of an error 

- restoration of a lost capability 

- receipt of authorization 

- restriction of authorization 

- change of goal 

- transition to a new task. 

A traditional trajectory looks like this: 

_Course 1 → Course 2 → Course 3 → Examination._ 

A quantum trajectory looks different: 

_Current state → identified gap → possible quantum → attempt → result → new state → new branch._ 

### 28. Every Student’s Graph Is Unique 

Even when two students are preparing for the same role, their trajectories should not coincide automatically. They differ in: 

- initial capabilities 

- prior experience 

- demonstrated actions 

- errors 

- rate of learning • available tools 

- level of assistance required 

   - employer requirements 

- professional goals 

   - admissible risk. 

- work context 

One student may need only one simulation. Another may require an explanation, a series of intermediate tasks, and work with a mentor. A third may already possess the required capability and should proceed directly to assessment. 

Assigning the same program to everyone means ignoring their actual states. 

### 29. The Map Is Shared; the Route Is Not 

An educational organization may maintain a shared map of the capability space: 

- which states exist • which quanta are available 

- which transitions are possible • which evidence is required 

- • which dependencies have been established • which requirements are imposed by roles and tasks. 

But the specific route is constructed individually. The common program is replaced by a common graph of possible transitions. An individual subgraph is constructed for each student. The shared map describes the available capability states, dependencies, and admissible transitions between them. 

The individual graph is the current subgraph of that map generated for a particular person at a particular moment in time. The individual graph depends on: 

- the student’s current state 

- the student’s goals 

   - available actors 

   - evidence 

- the requirements of the activity • the moment in time. 

It is not selected once. It is recomputed after every event.

### 36. The Economics of the Legacy Educational Unit 

The course, semester, and degree persist not only because of pedagogical inertia. They support a stable financial, administrative, and regulatory system. The course has real advantages: it coordinates large groups, creates a shared intellectual environment, supports socialization, and makes it possible to construct a foundational sequence. Quantum learning does not abolish the course as a possible pedagogical container. It rejects the course as the primary unit of outcome, payment, and accountability. 

The legacy system is rational relative to its own objectives—predictable funding, standardization, scheduling, reporting, and risk transfer. But these objectives are not identical to a demonstrated change in capability.

#### 36.1. The Diploma as a Social Signal and the Speed of the Market 

Historically, the diploma has performed not only an educational function, but also a signaling function. Prolonged completion of a selective program told the market that a person could comply with complex rules, sustain a demanding workload, belong to a particular environment, and pass through a social filter. This function is real; quantum learning does not deny it. 

But the economic value of a delayed signal depends on the speed of the world. While professions and technologies lasted for decades, an employer could use a diploma as a convenient but lazy proxy for general readiness. When work itself changes faster than the educational cycle, waiting five years becomes an excessively expensive way for the mass market to determine whether a person can perform a specific task today. 

_“The traditional university may continue to treat the diploma as a social filter. But the market will no longer wait five years for you to mature. Except in professions where the cost of error requires a long pathway, the employer needs evidence of capability here and now.”_ — Alexander Vityaz 

The market therefore increasingly separates two distinct forms of value. Community, networks, culture, initiation, and affiliation with a strong university may retain independent value. But they must no longer substitute for a qualification claim. Belonging to a group is one product; demonstrated capability is another. 

This shift is also reflected in anticipated hiring practices. According to the World Economic Forum, over the 2025–2030 horizon, 81% of employers expect to use work experience as a mechanism for assessing candidates, 48% expect to use direct skills assessments, and 43% expect to require a university degree [8]. The diploma does not disappear, but it ceases to be the only—or a sufficient—proxy for readiness to work. 

|Function|Logic of the diploma|Logic of quantum learning|
|---|---|---|
|Selection|Four or fve years as an indirect<br>signal of discipline, endurance, and<br>compliance with rules|Direct evidence of capability to<br>perform a specifc class of tasks|
|Speed|The signal appears only after the<br>entire program is completed|Evidence appears after each<br>completed transition|
|Community and network|Membership in a group and brand is<br>combined with the qualifcation<br>claim|Community remains a distinct value<br>but does not substitute for evidence<br>of capability|
|Long-cycle professions|Duration and credit hours serve as<br>the principal proxy for readiness|The long pathway remains, but<br>consists of a sequence of evidence,<br>simulations, and authorizations|
|Response to the market|The employer reassesses the graduate<br>after the diploma has been issued|The employer receives current<br>evidence in the stated context and can<br>see its limitations|

For the working adult, this shift is particularly clear. The person already has a professional history, responsibilities, a family, and limited time. Requiring another multiyear social ritual merely to obtain a new signal means ignoring both the person’s initial state and the speed of market change. The person needs a portable result from the next transition, not another initiation. The exception is not long-form university education as such, but transitions that objectively require prolonged maturation and repeated assessment: medicine, aviation, safety-critical engineering, fundamental science, and complex professional judgment. The quantum model need not shorten such pathways artificially. It makes them executable and traceable: thousands of connected quanta, simulations, observations, and graduated authorizations in place of one undifferentiated statement—“studied for five years.” 

The social signal is therefore neither prohibited nor administratively devalued. It simply ceases to count as sufficient evidence of professional readiness where the market already requires direct, current, and contextual confirmation of capability.

#### 37.4. The Quantum as an Outcome Contract and a Chain of Accountability 

Today, a diploma is portable as a document, but responsibility for the qualification claim is almost entirely non-portable. The student pays for a program, the university issues a diploma, and the employer reassesses everything. When the graduate cannot perform the work, the claim dissolves among the program, teacher, examination, and the employer’s hidden requirements. 

In the quantum system, every capability claim has a provenance: task, context, attempt, assistance used, result, criterion, assessor, validity period, and scope of applicability. This makes it possible to identify not an abstract guilty party, but the element of the regulatory circuit whose stated condition or obligation was not fulfilled. 

The provenance and responsibility model developed here extends the actor-accounting logic of _Management Debt_ , which links observable events, decisions, omissions, traces, materialized risks, and consequences to accountable actors ([Management Debt—Part I](../2026-management-debt-part-i/) [11]).

Accountability does not mean an absolute guarantee of employment success: a capability may weaken, the context may change, and a new task may fall outside the scope of the original evidence. But the system must show exactly what was promised, who assessed it, under what conditions, and why authorization was issued. 

A course sells access to content. Quantum learning sells a verified transition and makes responsibility for the evidence traceable. 

### 38. The Business Model of the Quantum University 

The quantum university ceases to depend solely on one large sale at the beginning of a person’s life. It becomes the operator of a continuous development graph: it diagnoses the state, recognizes what has already been demonstrated, supplies the necessary transitions, brings in experts and simulations, verifies the result, and returns when the technology, role, or goal changes again. 

The one-time fee for a degree may decline because the student no longer pays for the same complete package as everyone else. But longer relationships, new categories of payer, and recurring revenue emerge: the person returns for new transitions, employers purchase continuous employee development, and the state purchases verifiable reskilling pathways. 

#### 38.1. The Working Adult Is the Primary Learner of the Quantum University 

_“The primary learner in the modern university is no longer the recent school graduate, but the working adult. This person does not come once for a diploma, but returns throughout an entire professional life—for the next verified transition in capability.”_ — Alexander Vityaz 

The traditional university is designed primarily around a young person who can subordinate several years of life to the academic calendar. The quantum university is designed around an adult who already has an occupation, heterogeneous experience, and three recurring domains of responsibility: paid work, family—including care for loved ones and pets—and continuous learning. The person’s time is limited, and the new capability must be applied in a specific context. 

As Figure 1 shows, learning becomes one of several parallel circuits of responsibility for the working adult. The university’s principal constraint is therefore not merely access to content, but its capacity to reduce the time, cost, and organizational friction of the transition without damaging the person’s work, family, or recovery.

This change in audience produces a new operating model: year-round enrollment, short transition contracts, hybrid and asynchronous formats, recognition of prior experience, corporate subscriptions, shared funding, and billing based on the pathway actually completed. The university stops waiting for the next age cohort and begins supporting the economy already in operation.

The university acquires a portfolio of interconnected products: core infrastructure and foundational environments, diagnosis, quanta, simulations, mentoring, independent assessment, stewardship of the individual graph, and corporate contracts. A student becomes not a participant in one program, but a long-term user of the university at different stages of professional life. 

#### 38.3. One Teacher Supports More Students 

In the traditional model, a substantial share of the teacher’s time is spent repeating the same lectures, explanations, and assessments for successive cohorts. Scaling requires either enlarging the audience or increasing contact hours. In the quantum model, the teacher’s scarce expertise becomes a reusable intellectual asset. The teacher describes the capability, designs the quantum, creates the simulation scenario, and establishes the assessment criteria. AI and Simulator adapt the explanation, generate exercises, provide routine feedback, and route individual graphs. 

A human remains in the circuit wherever automation cannot replace professional judgment: complex diagnosis, mentoring, research, conflicts between assessments, and verification of critical capabilities. One teacher can therefore support many more students without turning learning into a mass broadcast of identical content. The limiting resource becomes not the size of the audience, but the number of complex interventions that genuinely require human expertise. This allows the teacher to support more students while preserving quality where quality cannot be scaled automatically.

|Line of business|What the university sells|
|---|---|

The economics of the quantum university combines high initial costs for designing a high-quality capability model, quantum, and simulation with a lower marginal cost for each subsequent use. A common foundational quantum may serve employees of many companies, while a role-specific layer is added on top. 

This model creates economies of scale, but also raises the importance of open standards and portability. If one platform holds the person’s evidence and graph captive, the efficiency gain becomes monopoly rent. Competition is possible only when the student and company can change providers while retaining demonstrated states and the history of accountability. 

### 39. The Role of the University 

Quantum learning does not mean the destruction of the university. It means the end of the university’s monopoly on preparing a person for professional life. The university may remain a space for: 

- fundamental knowledge 

- research 

- laboratories 

- scientific thought 

- complex intellectual transitions 

- independent assessment 

- the formation of capabilities that cannot be acquired directly in the work process. 

But the university must no longer be a diploma factory through which a person passes once at the beginning of life. It must become one of the actors in a continuous educational system. Its digital twin must show: 

- which transitions it can provide 

- for which students 

- through which teachers 

- under what conditions 

- at what cost 

- at what speed 

- with what durability of the result 

- on the basis of what evidence. 

#### 39.1. The University as an Operator of Long-Horizon and Exploratory Transitions 

The university must support all three circuits. In adaptation, it forms and independently verifies professional transitions. In development, it creates long-horizon environments for mastery and for scientific and professional judgment. In exploration, it preserves an autonomous space for knowledge that does not yet have a vacancy, a customer, or a known return on investment. 

Knowledge beyond immediate demand is not a defect. It creates optionality: the human capacity to formulate a question, build a new field, and change the graph of activity itself. The requirements of the current employer therefore cannot be the sole source of educational goals. 

#### 39.2. Transitions and Exploration Require Different Sources of Funding 

Payment for an individual pathway is applicable where the target state and sufficient evidence can be substantively defined. It must not turn the entire university into a supplier of short-term functions for current business. 

Fundamental knowledge, culture, research, and the creation of new fields are public and long-term infrastructure. They require core public funding, grants, endowments, research contracts, and internal cross-subsidization. Revenue from professional transitions and B2B services may strengthen this circuit, but must not replace it with the logic of immediate payback. 

The quantum university is a hybrid: transitions are funded as a demonstrable service; exploration is funded as the creation of a future space of possibilities. 

### 40. Why the Corporate University Does Not Solve the Problem 

Corporate universities, postgraduate programs, online platforms, and course libraries usually preserve the previous architecture. They still offer: 

- long programs • learning separated from work 

- • preassembled modules • verification of completion • a common pace • a certificate at the end. 

This is not a new educational system. It is the old system inside a company. A digital platform does not make learning modern when the course remains the primary unit. Personalizing the interface is not personalizing the transition. 

Reducing a lecture from one hour to fifteen minutes does not turn it into a learning quantum. 

#### 40.1. Digitizing Delivery Does Not Change the Educational Unit 

> _“Coursera and similar services are lipstick on a pig. They digitized an obsolete process and continue to sell the same product: a preassembled course, a program, and a qualification document at the end—only online.”_ — Alexander Vityaz 

Coursera and similar platforms have indeed solved an important delivery problem: they expanded access, reduced the marginal cost of distributing content, and made learning asynchronous and global. But Coursera’s official product portfolio remains organized around courses, Specializations, Professional Certificates, university certificates, and online degrees; its enterprise offering sells teams access to a catalog and analytics [7]. 

|What has been digitized|What has not changed|
|---|---|
|Delivery: the classroom has been replaced by a browser,<br>mobile application, and video|Primary unit: the course, specialization, certifcate, or<br>degree|
|Access: a global catalog, asynchronous participation, and<br>subscription|Pathway: preassembled by the provider rather than<br>computed from the state of a specifc person|
|Control: tests, automated grading, and completion<br>analytics|Result: completion and a credential, rather than<br>demonstrated action in a work context|
|Scale: one body of content serves millions of users|Economics: access to a package or seat is sold, rather<br>than the transition actually required|
|Enterprise product: a catalog and analytics for teams|Accountability: the connection among learning, evidence,<br>and real authorization remains open|

The mode of delivery has been digitized, but the object of the transaction has not. The user still purchases a preassembled educational package and receives a qualification document after completion. The platform knows what the person viewed and completed, but this alone does not demonstrate that the person can perform specific work in a specified context, at the required quality and level of independence. An online platform can become part of quantum learning, but only if it changes the unit: from a catalog of courses and credentials to gap diagnosis, an individual graph, simulation, evidence, and authorization. The criticism concerns neither the digital channel nor Coursera as a company, but the architecture of education that the digital channel has preserved. 

A university’s continuous B2B service is not simply a new name for a corporate university. Its unit is the individual verified transition rather than the company’s internal program. The university preserves the independence of assessment, works with multiple employers, reuses a common foundation, and returns actual results to its own quality model. 

## Part VI. Principles, Definition, and Manifesto 

### 41. Principles of Quantum Learning 

**Capability matters more than knowledge.** Knowledge has value when it enables a person to act, understand, create, or make a decision. **The transition matters more than the content.** Content is a means; the result is a change in the person’s state. **The event matters more than the schedule.** Learning begins when a task, goal, error, hypothesis, or new context emerges. **Action and evidence matter more than completion and certification.** A claimed capability must be supported by a relevant result. 

**The individual graph matters more than the common program.** Initial state, route, assistance, and evidence differ from one person to another. **The model and feedback matter more than administrative representation.** The digital twin is updated by events and corrected by actual results. **A recognized state matters more than repeated learning.** A capability already demonstrated reduces the time, cost, and risk of the next pathway. 

**Accountability matters more than a qualification declaration.** Every item of evidence has a provenance, scope of applicability, assessor, and review procedure. **A bounded and contestable model matters more than total observability.** Evidence of capability is portable; a permanent history of errors and drafts is not. 

Continuity includes adaptation, development, and exploration. For the working adult, learning becomes a third job—after paid employment and family. Time, cost, and support must therefore be designed as part of a professional and social system, not as a private hobby after the working day. 

### 42. Working Definition 

_Quantum learning is an event-driven system, implemented through digital twins, for managing minimal, verified transitions in human capability._ 

Every transition: 

- is triggered by an event 

- is connected to a specific gap 

- is executed as a distinct process 

- includes human action 

- records attempts and assistance 

- ends with assessment 

- creates evidence 

- changes the state of the digital twin 

- affects authorization for subsequent actions 

- restructures the individual learning graph. 

Quantum learning is not a complete theory of education and does not claim to provide a complete model of the human being. It is an architecture for managing demonstrable transitions in domains where the action, context, and sufficient evidence can be substantively defined. In development and exploration, the system performs a supportive rather than exhaustive function. In applied domains, basic target states may be assembled into a professional trivium—a compact list of tasks that a qualified specialist must be able to perform independently. Quanta construct the individual pathway to those tasks, and evidence shows that the necessary minimum has genuinely been attained. Quantum learning does not refer merely to: 

- microlearning 

- an adaptive test 

- an AI tutor 

   - a content library 

   - a digital diploma 

   - an updated LMS. 

- a personalized course 

It is an architecture in which all participants and consequential objects in the educational process are represented by digital twins, while human development is executed as an individual graph of verified transitions. 

### 43. Manifesto 

#### Unit and Outcome 

We demand that education measure changes in capability, not hours, credits, and the volume of content consumed. We demand that the mandatory common program be replaced by an individual graph beginning with the person’s current state and the requirements of a specific task or goal. 

We demand that every professionally consequential transition end in evidence and, where necessary, a transparent authorization decision. 

#### Actors and Execution 

We demand that all consequential participants and objects in the process be represented by digital twins in an executable graph of states, events, and accountability. We demand that the employer become an active participant by describing real tasks and risks, providing context, participating in assessment, and returning the actual result. 

We demand that AI be used to diagnose and adapt transitions, but never be granted unilateral authority to assign an irreversible status to a person. 

#### Economics and Accountability 

We demand that the student pay for the pathway actually required, and that recognized capabilities reduce its duration and price. We demand that the chain of the qualification claim be traceable—from the task model and diagnosis to the quantum, assessment, evidence, and authorization. 

We demand that the university become an operator of the continuous development of people and companies; that it design learning for the working adult as a third job compatible with paid employment and family; and that teacher expertise scale through reusable quanta, simulations, and work on complex cases. 

#### Rights and Openness 

We demand that the digital model be bounded, transparent, portable, and contestable, rather than becoming a universal rating of the person. We demand separation of the private learning history from portable evidence, data minimization, and defined retention periods for learning traces. 

We demand the preservation of autonomy in development and exploration: a known gap and current employer demand do not exhaust the purposes of education. 

### 44. The First Move: One Task, One Person, One Quantum 

Quantum learning does not require beginning with the reform of an entire university, industry, or company. The first operating circuit is built around one real task, one person, and one demonstrable transition. The employer describes the required result, work context, critical errors, and admissible risk. The university or another provider records the initial state, designs one quantum, and defines the assessment procedure. The person follows an individual pathway, while the teacher, mentor, expert, and AI enter only to the extent required for that transition. The circuit is closed not when the learning material has been completed, but when evidence has been created, an authorization decision made, and the actual work result returned to the digital twins of all participants. This minimum scale makes it possible to test the architecture, economics, access rights, and allocation of accountability before the system is expanded. 

##### **MINIMUM OPERATING CIRCUIT** 

ONE TASK _→_ ONE PERSON _→_ ONE QUANTUM _→_ ONE ITEM OF EVIDENCE _→_ ONE AUTHORIZATION 

## Conclusion 

The principal crisis of modern education lies not only in its duration. It lies in the mismatch between the form of the educational process and the speed at which activity changes. The system continues to transmit knowledge in large packages, while the need for new capabilities arises continuously, locally, and in response to events. Shortening a course, moving it online, or adding AI-generated content does not change the primary unit. At the labor-market level, the cost of the legacy unit appears as structural unemployment: vacancies and people coexist, but no demonstrable, safe, and economically admissible transition has been constructed between their graphs. 

Quantum learning becomes an adaptation layer for the labor market, but it does not reduce the person to a set of functions or subordinate all education to current business demand. The digital model remains bounded, learning errors remain protected, and the university preserves a circuit for long-horizon development and open exploration. The primary unit must change. In place of the course—a verified transition. In place of the common program—an individual graph. 

In place of completion and certification—action and evidence. In place of payment for the entire package—payment for the pathway actually required, with recognition of what has already been demonstrated. In place of a one-time sale of a degree—continuous stewardship of the development of the person and the company. In place of diffuse accountability—a traceable chain from the task and quantum to evidence and authorization. **A course measures content. A quantum measures change in the person.** 

Education must cease to be a period of life. It must become a continuous system of precise, timely, and verifiable transitions. 

This is quantum learning.
